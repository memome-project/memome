Inoculator

status: post-launch

This capability does not exist yet — and that is deliberate.

The inoculator deploys hardening content from the positive catalog into live agent state. It ships after the pilot and the v0.3 freeze, and only inside a sandbox. No timeline is promised here.

What it will be

The inoculator completes the product loop: scan → detect → harden → rescan. Where the scanner answers “are we contaminated?”, the inoculator deploys the fix — interventions from the hardening catalog written into agent memory, workspace, and configuration, then verified by the after-scan.

Its content uses an outcome-first evidence axis, mirroring the bad corpus's discipline:

  • Verified outcome — measured, with population/sample/method stated.
  • Observed-beneficial — real-world signal, not yet measured.
  • Suggested — explicitly labeled, never implied effective.

Why it's late on purpose

Writing patterns into live agent state is exactly the class of operation our sandbox policy (governance §9) was written for. The rule: detection and deployment tooling that touches real systems runs only in an isolated environment — and we do not ship that capability from a laptop. Publishing this status page is part of the discipline: we state publicly that it ships later, deliberately.

What you can do now

  • Try the Advisor — free, in your browser.
  • Browse the open corpus — the threat side, CC-BY-4.0.
  • Comment on the methodology and governance — the community weighs in on process and outcomes summaries; the intervention text itself is product content.

The hardening catalog is proprietary product content — the same relationship as MITRE ATT&CK (open) versus commercial detections and playbooks (closed). The methodology behind it is public and open to comment.
